# Security Foundations | Technical Review 10: Abuse, Deception, Threat Models, and Threat Hunting Models

**Review date:** November 19, 2026  
**Scope:** Two substantive area-first Drafts in [Threats, Hazards & Adversarial Behavior](threats-hazards-adversarial-behavior/README.md): **SF-03-04 Attacks, Deception, Abuse, and Misuse** and **SF-03-07 Threat Assumptions and Models**. **20 material claim groups**, nine and eleven respectively, checked independently after Draft Batch 10.  
**Method:** Separate model-assisted source/claim review against official OWASP, NIST, CAPEC, Microsoft, W3C and MITRE sources; differentiation of source-defined versus editorial categories; visually inspected MITRE's TTP-Based Hunting Figure 3 (printed p. 10 / PDF page index 11); verified NIST SP 800-154's draft status and W3C's 2026 Group Note Draft status; checked formal-model entailment limitations and independently recalculated a conditional-probability example; applied bounded corrections to both manuscripts before their review-status transition.  
**Disposition:** The **corrected** manuscripts satisfy **Reviewed (model-assisted) / Core claims checked**, not Source-Verified, independent human peer acceptance, final taxonomy approval, proof of hunting efficacy, legal judgment of misuse, or Published status.

## 1. SF-03-04 | Attacks, Deception, Abuse, and Misuse

**Manuscript:** [Attacks, Deception, Abuse, and Misuse](threats-hazards-adversarial-behavior/attacks-deception-abuse-and-misuse.md)  
**Source basis:** [OWASP Abuse Case Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Abuse_Case_Cheat_Sheet.html); [OWASP Abuse of Functionality](https://community.owasp.org/attacks/Abuse_of_Functionality); [NIST social engineering glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/social_engineering); [MITRE CAPEC-407 Pretexting](https://capec.mitre.org/data/definitions/407.html) and [CAPEC-98 Phishing](https://capec.mitre.org/data/definitions/98.html); [Microsoft STRIDE threat categories](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/security/develop/threat-modeling-tool-threats); [OWASP Threat Modeling](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Threat_Modeling_Cheat_Sheet.html); [NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final).

| Group | Material proposition | Finding, correction and important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10-04-01 | Attack, attack attempt, security violation, compromise and harm are distinct | **Pass.** Intentional attempted violation does not guarantee successful disclosure, compromise or realized harm. The article's definitions are **working security-analytic distinctions**, not verbatim universal glossary categories; context and evidence are necessary to claim actual outcome or actor intent. |
| 10-04-02 | Misuse includes accidental errors in the article, while OWASP abuse cases are attacker-oriented | **Pass after correction.** OWASP [Abuse Case](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Abuse_Case_Cheat_Sheet.html) explicitly defines feature use from an *attacker action/input* perspective. A broader class of policy-inconsistent *misuse* can include honest error, but **that generalization is this repository's editorial distinction**, not OWASP's official definition of an abuse case. The manuscript now says so. |
| 10-04-03 | Legitimate functionality can be harmful without a conventional software exploit | **Pass with caveat.** [OWASP Abuse of Functionality](https://community.owasp.org/attacks/Abuse_of_Functionality) explicitly describes valid features used unexpectedly **and** cases involving business-logic or insufficient anti-automation weaknesses. Corrected the overbroad implication that all feature abuse is vulnerability-free. Technical success of a valid request does not prove its legitimacy. |
| 10-04-04 | Deception requires a deliberate misleading mechanism, not merely erroneous output | **Pass after clarification.** A mistaken system output might lead to a bad decision without intentional deception. The corrected article distinguishes **adversarial deliberate misrepresentation** from unintended misleading records, while allowing automated systems to accept false representations without anthropomorphizing their “beliefs.” |
| 10-04-05 | Social engineering, pretexting and phishing overlap but are not synonyms | **Pass.** NIST's [social engineering glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/social_engineering) shows **multiple source-attributed definitions** (including SP 800-82 Rev. 3 and SP 800-63-4). [CAPEC-407](https://capec.mitre.org/data/definitions/407.html) describes invented identity/situation to influence a target; [CAPEC-98](https://capec.mitre.org/data/definitions/98.html) describes a particular phishing pattern. These taxonomy entries neither prove a real event nor encompass all forms of deceptive influence. |
| 10-04-06 | Valid authentication is not identical to justified authorization, purpose or consent | **Pass.** A valid session may permit a transaction while the purpose of repeated use, sharing or distribution violates a separately stated policy. Whether the result is a security violation depends on the **actual policy, authority and affected interest**, not the analyst's impression of unusual behavior. |
| 10-04-07 | STRIDE is a threat-identification prompt set, not an intent or harm ontology | **Pass with clarification.** Microsoft lists **Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service and Elevation of Privilege**. Repudiation concerns a claimed action that cannot be adequately proved, not evidence that a particular person denied responsibility. STRIDE's six categories are not an exhaustive taxonomy of social-engineering motivations or privacy harms. |
| 10-04-08 | OWASP misuse/abuse case is a model of possible feature use, not evidence of occurrence | **Pass.** The OWASP abuse-case document is pragmatic design guidance. The fictional document-invitation scenario is a counterexample to equating permitted program behavior with legitimate intent, not a report of harassment, unauthorized access, an attacker or actual harm. |
| 10-04-09 | Behavioral classification, actor attribution and harm evidence are separate owners | **Pass.** SF-03-04 owns *types of conduct and misuse of expected features*. SF-03-03 owns capability and intent hypotheses, SF-03-05 owns ATT&CK's behavior vocabulary, SF-03-07 owns models/assumptions, SF-10-08 owns evidence/attribution and SF-02-05 owns consequences. No new duplicate or mandatory operational workshop is introduced. |

**Material revisions applied:** Six bounded changes, including the OWASP-centered-versus-broad-misuse distinction, recognition of business-logic weaknesses within feature abuse, the boundary between deliberate deception and accidental misrepresentation, behavior-versus-policy evidence, and STRIDE's proper repudiation meaning. No person-specific threat attribution or actionable attack procedure was introduced.

## 2. SF-03-07 | Threat Assumptions and Models

**Manuscript:** [Threat Assumptions and Models](threats-hazards-adversarial-behavior/threat-assumptions-and-models.md)  
**Source basis:** [NIST threat-modeling glossary](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/threat_modeling), source-attributed to SP 800-53 Rev. 5; [NIST SP 800-154](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/154/ipd) (Initial Public Draft); [OWASP Threat Modeling](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Threat_Modeling_Cheat_Sheet.html); [W3C Threat Modeling Guide](https://www.w3.org/TR/threat-modeling-guide/) (Group Note Draft June 23, 2026); [MITRE TTP-Based Hunting report](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/prs-19-3892-ttp-based-hunting.pdf); [MITRE hunting training](https://attack.mitre.org/resources/learn-more-about-attack/training/threat-hunting/); [MITRE Cyber Analytics Repository glossary](https://car.mitre.org/resources/glossary/).

| Group | Material proposition | Finding, correction and important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10-07-01 | Threat modeling describes attack/defense of a scoped logical entity | **Pass.** NIST's glossary explicitly attributes its definition to **SP 800-53 Rev. 5** and includes data, application, host, system and environment. A particular threat model has explicit scope, assumptions and purpose; this definition is not a proof that every design-modeling method has one required schema. |
| 10-07-02 | Threat Models and Threat Hunting Models are different analytical purposes, not mandatory disjoint standard-defined types | **Pass after correction.** The article now explicitly labels this distinction a **repository conceptual separation**. A Threat Model represents potential security violations and system conditions; a Threat Hunting Model represents behavioral/evidence hypotheses and how they can be examined. Neither NIST nor MITRE is claimed to have ratified a universal two-class ontology. |
| 10-07-03 | Hunting model versus hunting methodology | **Pass after material correction.** MITRE's [TTP-Based Hunting report](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/prs-19-3892-ttp-based-hunting.pdf) calls its six connected activity steps a **methodology**. **Figure 3, printed p. 10 / PDF page index 11, visually inspected**, relates malicious-activity characterization, hypotheses, abstract analytics, data requirements, collection gaps and hunting execution. The **behavior/evidence model within the method** must not be conflated with the entire operational process. |
| 10-07-04 | CAR defines analytic, hypothesis and implementation at different levels | **Pass.** The official [MITRE Cyber Analytics Repository glossary](https://car.mitre.org/resources/glossary/) describes an analytic as identifying **observable behavior** generated for a TTP, with a hypothesis about expected observable behavior and an implementation in a particular platform. That supports the model/hypothesis/analytic distinction without guaranteeing detection effectiveness. |
| 10-07-05 | OWASP and W3C guidance includes broader harm and misuse contexts | **Pass with source status.** [OWASP](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Threat_Modeling_Cheat_Sheet.html) acknowledges multiple modeling approaches, not one universal procedure. The [W3C Group Note Draft](https://www.w3.org/TR/threat-modeling-guide/), **June 23, 2026**, includes privacy, abuse, ecosystem and people-related harms but explicitly states Group Note Drafts are **not endorsed by W3C or its Members**. The corrected manuscript preserves this draft status. |
| 10-07-06 | NIST SP 800-154 remains a draft despite planning-to-finalize notice | **Pass.** The official [SP 800-154 publication page](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/154/ipd) still labels **Initial Public Draft (March 2016)** and its January 23, 2025 note states a plan to finalize, *not* a final publication. This cannot be cited as current mandatory guidance. |
| 10-07-07 | Shared ATT&CK, Diamond, UKC, STRIDE and attack-tree models do not make their uses equivalent | **Pass.** Each has a different unit or purpose. A Diamond intrusion-event model can support evidence interpretation, ATT&CK represents tactic/technique categories, and STRIDE prompts possible design threats. Using one named model in both threat modeling and hunting does not transfer an incident proof, completeness assertion, or actor identity. |
| 10-07-08 | Formal model entailment differs from informal confidence and deployed-system truth | **Pass after correction.** \((M,A)\models G\) is a semantic entailment claim for **formally defined semantics** and justified proof/checking, not an automatic result of a coherent architecture diagram. The article now distinguishes entailment for a model from \((S)\models G\) for the real system and its abstraction/implementation fidelity obligations. |
| 10-07-09 | Abstraction overapproximation/underapproximation affects the direction of an inference | **Pass with qualification.** An overapproximation may introduce **spurious counterexamples**, whereas an underapproximation may miss real behaviors and falsely suggest a property holds everywhere. To carry a universal absence claim to reality, the abstraction must cover relevant real behavior and preserve the property in the appropriate direction. The manuscript makes the evidence boundary explicit. |
| 10-07-10 | Evidence predicted by a hypothesis is not equivalent to certainty after a match | **Recomputed.** Under an explicitly **fictional** model with \(P(H)=.02\), \(P(O\mid H)=.80\), \(P(O\mid\neg H)=.10\), Bayes gives \(P(H\mid O)=.016/(.016+.098)=.016/.114=0.14035087719\), approximately **14.04%**. Thus a positive analytic is not automatically a proven threat. This number is neither measured base rate nor a general hunting confidence score. |
| 10-07-11 | Export-worker example and ownership boundary are bounded | **Pass.** A public API's object authorization rule does not certify a background export path not included in the model; a proposed hunt needs appropriate observable evidence and legitimate alternatives. Merely discovering the worker does not prove a vulnerability or an incident. SF-03-07 owns the shared **assumptions/model-validity concept**; applied Threat Hunting Models and operational threat-modeling procedures belong to [Operational Foundations](../operational-foundations/README.md), without automatically creating a new formally approved operational subject. |

**Material revisions applied:** Seven editorial/technical corrections: marked the Threat Model versus Threat Hunting Model distinction as non-exhaustive and editorial; separated MITRE's hunting **methodology** from the underlying behavior/evidence **model**; restricted formal entailment to defined semantics; clarified over/underapproximation validity; added a 2%-prior conditional-inference counterexample; strengthened W3C draft-status authority caveats; and documented the inspected MITRE Figure 3 source location.

## Independent worked inference check

For the **hypothetical** hunt analytic, define:

\[
P(H)=0.02,\quad P(\neg H)=0.98,\quad
P(O\mid H)=0.80,\quad P(O\mid\neg H)=0.10.
\]

The total probability of an analytic match is \(P(O)=.80(.02)+.10(.98)=.114\). Hence:

\[
P(H\mid O)
=\frac{P(O\mid H)P(H)}{P(O)}
=\frac{.016}{.114}
\approx0.14035087719.
\]

A true-hypothesis detection rate of **80%** does not imply an **80% positive predictive value**. Under the stated population prior and false-match rate, a positive analytic corresponds to about **14.04%** posterior probability. This exercise checks probability algebra only, not detection performance, incident attribution or a universal Bayesian practice for analysts.

A **model-proof counterexample** is separate. Suppose an architectural model contains only the public document API. Proving that every modeled read is authorized does not establish the same property for a real service with an omitted export worker. Likewise, observing an export record in a hunt is not itself proof of illicit tenant access. The example requires the model-to-system and evidence-to-incident claims to be evaluated separately.

## Source authority and incomplete gates

- **Final NIST source:** [SP 800-53 Rev. 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/glossary/term/threat_modeling) for the glossary's model definition; [SP 800-30 Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/30/r1/final) for risk source/event/context framing.
- **Unfinalized NIST source:** [SP 800-154 (Initial Public Draft, March 2016)](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/154/ipd); the 2025 planning note is not final publication.
- **Unfinalized W3C source:** [Threat Modeling Guide, Group Note Draft (June 23, 2026)](https://www.w3.org/TR/threat-modeling-guide/), explicitly unendorsed by W3C/Members.
- **Published MITRE report:** [TTP-Based Hunting](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/2021-11/prs-19-3892-ttp-based-hunting.pdf), **dated March 2019 with 2020 copyright**, Figure 3 inspected at printed p. 10/PDF index 11; distinguish source-described process from the model used in it.
- **Living community/catalog sources:** OWASP abuse and threat-modeling guides; MITRE CAPEC current attack patterns; Microsoft STRIDE; MITRE ATT&CK training and CAR definitions. Their content may evolve and their category labels do not independently prove a security event.

**Not completed:** Full claim-to-passage verification of every statement; approval of a new Security Foundations or Operational Foundations taxonomy; independent qualified expert review; measurement of actual hunting detection statistics; investigation of real user data; historical incident attribution; a formal proof of actual system security; publication authorization. A structural CI pass confirms repository consistency, not the scientific correctness of a model.

# Security Foundations | Technical Review 06: Randomness, Nonces, and Cryptographic Key Lifecycles

**Review date:** November 18, 2026  
**Scope:** Two substantive area-first Draft manuscripts: [SF-09-02, Randomness, Entropy, and Nonces](cryptography-information-protection/randomness-entropy-and-nonces.md) and [SF-09-07, Key Lifecycle and Cryptographic Failures](cryptography-information-protection/key-lifecycle-and-cryptographic-failures.md). **19 material claim groups** (nine for SF-09-02 and ten for SF-09-07).  
**Method:** A separate, model-assisted second pass after the Draft batch. Compared major claims with original NIST publication records and terminology, IETF RFC 8439/5116/5869 technical passages, and NIST SP 800-57 Rev. 5 / SP 800-90B rendered PDF pages. Recomputed entropy, collision and counter-exhaustion examples; tested counterexamples to key/nonce and key-hierarchy claims; corrected the manuscripts before promotion.  
**Disposition:** The revised manuscripts pass the **bounded model-assisted technical review** recorded below. They may advance to **Reviewed (model-assisted) / Core claims checked**; neither is fully source-Verified, independently expert-reviewed, taxonomically ratified, empirically audited in a live system, or Published. A green structural check would not replace this claim-specific review.

## 1. SF-09-02: Randomness, Entropy, and Nonces

**Article:** [Randomness, Entropy, and Nonces](cryptography-information-protection/randomness-entropy-and-nonces.md)  
**Primary source orientation:** [NIST SP 800-90A Rev. 1](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/90/a/r1/final) (2015 final); [NIST SP 800-90B](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/90/b/final) (2018 final, 2025 errata notice); [NIST SP 800-90C](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/90/c/final) (2025 final); [IETF RFC 5116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5116.html) §§2,3; [IETF RFC 8439](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8439.html) §§2.6,2.8,4; [IETF RFC 5869](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5869.html) §3.1.

| Group | Reviewed proposition | Review result and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 06-02-01 | Randomness, entropy, unpredictability, uniqueness, freshness, and secrecy are not synonyms | **Pass.** The article correctly distinguishes distributional properties from actor-relative prediction and scope-relative uniqueness. A predictable counter may satisfy one nonce-uniqueness requirement but not secret-key unpredictability. A challenge's freshness and anti-replay obligations also require protocol-defined conditions. These are conceptual distinctions, not a universal nonce taxonomy specified by NIST. |
| 06-02-02 | The SP 800-90 series has distinct, current source responsibilities | **Pass.** NIST's [publication inventory](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/random-bit-generation/publications) identifies **90A Rev. 1 (2015 final)** for DRBGs, **90B (2018 final)** for entropy sources, and **90C (September 25, 2025 final)** for complete RBG constructions, including RBG1, RBG2, RBG3, and RBGC classes. SP 800-90A Rev. 2 remains a **pre-draft call** (September 2025), not a final standard. SP 800-90B lists two possible errata (May 2025), not an already superseding final revision. |
| 06-02-03 | Entropy-source health tests do not simply demand unbiased IID output | **Pass with clarification.** NIST SP 800-90B §4.1 **printed p. 22 / PDF index 29**, visually inspected, says raw noise often is not unbiased IID; source-specific health tests detect abnormal operation. §6 (especially §6.3, printed p. 41 / PDF index 48) distinguishes non-IID estimators and the most-common-value min-entropy estimate. Added this explicit limitation. A passing SP 800-22-like output test does not validate a source's claimed entropy. |
| 06-02-04 | Shannon entropy and min-entropy of the biased-bit model | **Recomputed.** For independent Bernoulli \(X\) with \(P(1)=0.75\), \(H(X)=-.75\log_2 .75-.25\log_2 .25=0.8112781244591328\) bits. \(H_\infty(X)=-\log_2(.75)=0.4150374992788438\) bits. The most likely one-sample guess has probability .75. Clarified that these are **known-distribution mathematical values**, not empirical lower confidence bounds or a NIST 90B validation result. |
| 06-02-05 | Deterministic expansion and independence of samples | **Pass with assumptions.** Under stipulated IID samples, the most likely \(n\)-sample vector has probability \(.75^n\) and min-entropy \(nH_\infty(X)\); correlations or attacker side information invalidate naïve addition. A deterministic transform of publicly known data cannot create unpredictable secret material merely by enlarging its representation. |
| 06-02-06 | DRBG operation does not independently certify its seeding or whole-system secrecy | **Pass.** NIST 90A describes deterministic generators; 90B covers source evidence; 90C combines such components in defined constructions. Duplicate seed/state, snapshot rollback and state disclosure may undermine intended goals. The article does not claim all DRBGs possess identical reseed or state-compromise guarantees. |
| 06-02-07 | Nonce, IV, counter, salt, challenge, and key obligations depend on their algorithms | **Pass.** RFC 5116 §§2–3 requires per-key nonce distinctness for its nonzero-length nonce AEAD interface and describes coordinated fixed-field/counter formation. RFC 8439 §§2.6, 2.8, 4 requires ChaCha20-Poly1305 nonce uniqueness and discourages random nonce generation as a substitute for guaranteed distinctness. RFC 5869 §3.1 describes nonsecret HKDF salt and its role. The article now explicitly attributes the stronger *no-random-generation* instruction to RFC 8439, not every AEAD. |
| 06-02-08 | Uniform random nonce collision illustration | **Recomputed.** For \(n=64,\ q=2^{32}\), the approximation \(1-\exp[-q(q-1)/2^{65}]\) gives **0.3934693402167571**, about **39.35%** chance of at least one duplicate under independent uniform draws. This is not the chance of repetition from a correctly persisted monotonic counter or of a non-uniform source with unknown dependence. The exact probability uses a product over the space of \(2^{64}\) values and was not numerically iterated term by term. |
| 06-02-09 | Nonce-reuse algebra, cryptographic consequences, and owner boundaries | **Pass.** Under a repeated keystream \(Z\), \(C_1=P_1\oplus Z,\ C_2=P_2\oplus Z\) implies \(C_1\oplus C_2=P_1\oplus P_2\). RFC 8439 §4 specifically notes repeated one-time Poly1305 key and keystream on repeated \((K,N)\). Those are construction-specific effects, not proof of a past compromise or an identical consequence for all AEADs. SF-09-02 owns random/nonce-input meaning, while SF-09-07 owns key lifecycle and SF-09-09 owns protocol composition. |

**Corrections applied to SF-09-02:** Explicitly distinguished SP 800-90B source-health and non-IID validation from general output statistics; qualified the biased-bit calculations as values of a stipulated distribution; attributed RFC 8439's stronger counter/nonce rule to its actual algorithm; reinforced key-scoped multi-sender state uniqueness; added precise source-location and errata caveats. No forced split or new canonical subject was justified.

## 2. SF-09-07: Key Lifecycle and Cryptographic Failures

**Article:** [Key Lifecycle and Cryptographic Failures](cryptography-information-protection/key-lifecycle-and-cryptographic-failures.md)  
**Primary source orientation:** [NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/57/pt1/r5/final) (2020 final); [NIST SP 800-133 Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/133/r2/final) (2020 final); [NIST SP 800-131A Rev. 2](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/131/a/r2/final) (2019 final); [NIST IR 8547](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/final) (October 27, 2026 final); [RFC 5116](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5116.html) §3.2; [RFC 5869](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5869.html).

| Group | Reviewed proposition | Review result and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 06-07-01 | Cryptographic keys have roles, dependencies, authority and protection boundaries | **Pass.** NIST SP 800-57 Rev. 5 treats types of keying material, functions and protections separately. Signing and verification keys differ in confidentiality and use requirements. A key label or complete inventory cannot by itself establish current secrecy. The article's dimensional table is editorial, not a verbatim NIST classification. |
| 06-07-02 | Cryptoperiod is not automatically the period when new encryption may begin | **Pass after correction.** Visually inspected NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 **§5.3.5, printed pp. 37–38 / PDF page indexes 49–50**. It distinguishes the **originator-usage period** (new protection) from the potentially longer **recipient-usage period** (processing past protected material), and defines the total symmetric-key cryptoperiod across the relevant span. Corrected the manuscript's oversimplified initial cryptoperiod definition. |
| 06-07-03 | Data re-encryption and key rewrapping can change different layers | **Pass after material correction.** If \(K_0\) directly encrypted content, rewrapping an unrelated DEK does **not** stop that content from depending on \(K_0\). In envelope encryption, replacing a wrapping key that protected DEKs can change the wrapping without changing ciphertext encrypted under those DEKs. This is a role-dependent derivation from standard cryptographic semantics; neither means an attacker loses historical copies. The manuscript now separates these cases explicitly. |
| 06-07-04 | A fixed per-key nonce counter can exhaust before scheduled calendar rotation | **Recomputed.** A fictional 32-bit counter has \(2^{32}=4,294,967,296\) distinct values. At exactly 1,000 uses/s: \(2^{32}/1000=4,294,967.296\) s = **49.71026962962963 days**. A 60-day calendar rotation would permit wraparound if the same key/prefix continues generating at that rate. [RFC 5116 §3.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5116.html) describes a fixed-field/counter design, but the article's specific prefix and usage rate are invented and do not establish compliance with any protocol. |
| 06-07-05 | Rotation, revocation, erasure, recovery and cryptographic restoration are different claims | **Pass.** An old key may remain in backups, the trust decision for certificates depends on relying parties, and a new KEK does not erase attacker-controlled historical material. The conceptual matrix preserves what each action can and cannot establish, without turning the article into an incident-response procedure. |
| 06-07-06 | HKDF derivation depends on suitable input material and domain-separated context | **Pass.** [RFC 5869 §§2–3](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc5869.html) defines extract/expand, a nonsecret salt, and \`info\` context. Reusing identical inputs, salt and context reproduces identical deterministic outputs. Neither KDF output length nor a public salt creates initial input secrecy. The article's key schedule is an explanatory schematic, not executable production protocol code. |
| 06-07-07 | Standards versions must distinguish current final text from proposals | **Pass with addition.** NIST **SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 final** versus **Rev. 6 Initial Public Draft (December 2025)**; **SP 800-133 Rev. 2 final** versus **Rev. 3 Initial Public Draft (April 2026)**; **SP 800-131A Rev. 2 final** versus **Rev. 3 draft (October 2024)**. Verified against the [NIST key-management publication inventory](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/key-management/publications). Added SP 800-133 Rev. 3 to the article's version caveats. |
| 06-07-08 | KEM and signature algorithm security purposes cannot be interchanged | **Pass.** [FIPS 203](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final) specifies ML-KEM; [FIPS 204](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/204/final) specifies ML-DSA signatures; [FIPS 205](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/205/final) specifies SLH-DSA signatures, all final August 2024. FIPS 203's official page identifies an issue for future correction, which does not mean its currently published final edition is silently updated. Protocol authentication and use constraints remain separately justified. |
| 06-07-09 | NIST IR 8547 is a final 2026 transition roadmap, not a deployment certification | **Pass.** The [official publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/final) identifies **October 27, 2026** as its final publication date. It maps quantum-vulnerable public-key standards toward post-quantum alternatives and contains a U.S. federal transition context. It is not evidence of successful migration at any specific organization or of present-day compromise by quantum computation. |
| 06-07-10 | Canonical owner and assurance limitations | **Pass.** SF-09-07 concerns key lifecycle, trust, failures and protection dependencies; SF-09-02 covers entropy and nonce generation; SF-09-09 owns full cryptographic protocol composition; operational key-rotation/response workflows belong in Operational Foundations. The article's diagrams, counter model and recovery considerations do not constitute a real-world assessment or source-Verified standard implementation. |

**Corrections applied to SF-09-07:** Rewrote the cryptoperiod definition around distinct originator and recipient usage and cited inspected NIST printed pages; corrected the direct-content-key versus envelope-wrapping-key case; added current **SP 800-133 Rev. 3 Initial Public Draft** status while retaining Rev. 2 as final; clarified that the four-octet RFC 5116 nonce-counter pattern is source-specific and does not override other limits; updated the source register. No new area or subject was justified.

## Independent arithmetic and counterexamples

The following calculations were performed independently of the manuscript text:

| Model | Inputs and calculation | Result / what is not established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biased independent bit | \(p=.75,\ H_2(p)=-p\log_2p-(1-p)\log_2(1-p)\) | \(H_2=.8112781244591328\) bits/sample; **not** a measured entropy-source lower bound |
| Maximum-probability guess | \(H_\infty=-\log_2(.75)\) | \(H_\infty=.4150374992788438\) bits/sample; under IID samples, min-entropy scales with count; correlated samples require separate analysis |
| Uniform nonce birthday | \(q=2^{32}, N=2^{64};\ 1-\exp[-q(q-1)/(2N)]\) | \(0.3934693402167571\), approximately **39.35%**; not valid for persistent non-repeating counters |
| Fixed 32-bit nonce counter | \(2^{32}/1000\) seconds at 1,000 invocations/s | \(4,294,967.296\) seconds ≈ **49.71027 days**, before a 60-day scheduled rotation if key/prefix unchanged |
| Repeated keystream | \(C_1=P_1\oplus Z,\ C_2=P_2\oplus Z\) | \(C_1\oplus C_2=P_1\oplus P_2\); algebraic model, not proof of real compromise |
| Envelope key rotation | Replace KEK on wrapped DEKs without replacing DEKs themselves | Changes wrapping; **does not** automatically re-encrypt content or erase previously captured KEK/DEK copies |
| Direct encryption key rotation | Data encrypted directly under \(K_0\), switch new writes to \(K_1\) | Does **not** make old data independent of \(K_0\) without genuine re-encryption or another justified migration |

The birthday formula is an approximation based on independent uniformly distributed draws. The exact finite-space collision probability is expressed in the article as a product; enumerating all \(2^{32}\) factors is unnecessary for the validated approximation, whose neglected higher-order term is tiny at this scale.

## Source evidence, limits and approval boundary

**Rendered source pages inspected:** NIST SP 800-90B §4.1 (PDF index 29, printed p. 22) on non-IID raw noise and technology-specific health testing; §6.3 (PDF index 48, printed p. 41) on statistical min-entropy estimation; NIST SP 800-57 Part 1 Rev. 5 §5.3.5 (PDF indexes 49–50, printed pp. 37–38) on symmetric-key usage and total cryptoperiod. Publication-version status was checked against NIST's live final/draft publication records, while RFC 8439, RFC 5116 and RFC 5869 source text supplied nonce and key-derivation claims.

**Not performed:** A full clause-by-clause audit of every manuscript statement, all proposed NIST draft revisions or 90B errata details; independent qualified human review; formal cryptographic proof; entropy-source validation in a physical device; deployed-system key-rotation or post-quantum migration testing; external audit or publication acceptance.

## Meaning of ownership

The single-owner rule prevents a security property or threat concept from being defined in multiple contradictory locations. Related concepts may be linked from different areas, but the same underlying definition should be maintained once. A **working owner** is not a final approved ontological class or standards mandate.

[Fundamentals](../fundamentals/README.md) owns generic mathematics, science, computing, systems and evidence; [Operational Foundations](../operational-foundations/README.md) owns security-work methods; the seven specialized domains own their functional implementations.

## Historical consolidation and review provenance

The original **SF-02-06** candidate was consolidated into **SF-02-05**, with scenario risk estimates remaining in **SF-05-03**. The former **SF-03-08** attribution concept was divided between **SF-03-03** (actor goals/intent hypotheses) and **SF-10-08** (evidentiary attribution). These mappings are historical, **not additional active subjects**, and are recorded in [Architecture Adjudication 02](ARCHITECTURE-ADJUDICATION-02.md).

The [area-structure decision](AREA-STRUCTURE-ADJUDICATION-03.md) approved **where working documents live**, not permanent acceptance of the listed subject names or the formal meaning of their relationships. [Development Register](DEVELOPMENT-VERIFICATION-REGISTER.md) contains the independently tracked source/development state for each article.

## Change contract

On each justified change: identify the original concept and intended security claim; compare existing owner and source models; choose **Deepen, Add, Split, Merge, Relocate, Cross-link or Defer**; preserve old IDs/paths; update area README and main inventory; review changed claims; rerun structural validation. Avoid extra articles for model names that fit within an established owner. Numbers remain flexible.
